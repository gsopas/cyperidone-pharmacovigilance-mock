## Clinical Narrative

A 52-year-old male patient with paranoid schizophrenia on maintenance therapy was commenced on Cyperidone 1 mg daily on 10 February 2025, titrated to 4 mg daily over two weeks. Baseline ECG prior to initiation showed normal sinus rhythm with QTc 418 ms (Bazett correction).

The patient had a routine cardiology review on 23 April 2025 (Day 72) prompted by the treating psychiatrist as a precautionary measure given the family history of sudden cardiac death. The 12-lead ECG showed sinus rhythm with QTc 478 ms, representing a 60 ms increase from baseline.

The cardiologist assessed the QTc prolongation as clinically significant given the family history, although the value remained below the 500 ms threshold that typically mandates drug discontinuation. Cyperidone dose was reduced from 4 mg to 2 mg daily and a repeat ECG was scheduled for 2 weeks post dose reduction.

Follow-up ECG on 05 May 2025 showed QTc 452 ms, representing a partial improvement from the peak value but still elevated above baseline. The cardiologist recommended continued monitoring with ECG every 4 weeks and avoidance of any concomitant QTc-prolonging medications.

Cyperidone was not discontinued as the treating psychiatrist assessed the benefit of continued antipsychotic therapy as outweighing the cardiac risk at the reduced dose level, particularly given the patient's limited treatment options.

## Causality Assessment

Assessed using the WHO-UMC causality assessment system.

**Assessment: Possible**

Justification:
- Reasonable temporal relationship (onset at Day 72)
- Consistent with known pharmacology: QTc prolongation is a listed adverse reaction for the atypical antipsychotic class at common frequency
- Partial positive response to dose reduction (QTc decreased from 478 to 452 ms)
- However, the family history of sudden cardiac death raises the possibility of an underlying genetic predisposition to QTc prolongation (e.g. subclinical long QT syndrome) that may confound the assessment
- Concomitant amlodipine is not known to prolong QTc
- Assessment downgraded from "probable" to "possible" due to the confounding cardiac family history
